Novel figure pipeline · current final · Misaka Mikoto

Misaka as a triangle hologram, with a skirt that moves

The figure from trial 2, before the separate clothing system: one skeleton with the summer and winter uniforms as whole bodies to swap, now wearing the detailed head made from a high-resolution portrait. One-shot + soft layers (the default) keeps the one-shot figure whole and puts its generated base body underneath as a hidden layer: each point's distance from that body is its layer thickness, so skin and tight cloth stay on the skeleton while thick cloth (the skirt, hems) hangs on soft springs from where the skeleton puts it, swings and settles, stretches at most 25% between neighbours, and is pushed out of capsules around the legs and torso; Layers in Look colours the triangles from tight (cyan) to loose (pink). Rigid needles (v0.6, the default) moves the base body by dual-quaternion blending (rotation about each joint, so a bent hip keeps its volume), rides the skin on it, and never lets the floating layer sink below 80% of its own depth; Plain skinning is v0.5. Base body + clothes puts a separately generated base body under the clothes, fitted bone by bone to the benchmark's proportions; the clothes are the benchmark's own, and the skirt hangs from the hips on ten spring-bone chains pushed aside by colliders on the legs, so the legs bend freely and a swinging skirt shows real thighs. Skirt springs only is the same skirt on the fused figure; Benchmark is the saved version without either. Every motion plays on the standard VRM humanoid bones, so it works on any figure made this way. The clothing system below is parked as work in progress.

How the clothes come off the body

  • The base body is the character in a plain fitted base layer, made once, with the portrait head on it.
  • The diffusion model's drawings changed her proportions (limbs by 2–13%, shoulders and hips by up to 14%), so its bodies are thrown away: each outfit model is rigged with the same 21 bones and its clothes are moved bone by bone onto the base skeleton, each bone's piece stretched to the base bone's length.
  • Cloth that ends up inside the body or touching it is pushed out to 4 mm; loose cloth is then settled by a cloth simulation with the base body as the collider.
  • The clothes are whatever stands out from the base body: more than 6 mm along its surface, or more than 2 cm away in any direction.
  • The clothes take the body's skin weights from the point beneath; loose cloth follows the hips more than the legs, and a long robe hangs from the hips.
  • The body parts each outfit covers (24–37% of the body) are hidden, so nothing pokes through the cloth.

What is left to fix

  • The furisode's hem still pulls a strip toward the back sandal in a long stride. Robes should tell the motion layer to take shorter steps, as a kimono does.
  • The coat came out with its hood up; only its lower part survives as a collar, since every outfit uses the shared head.
  • The black mourning kimono (Genesis 12–13) needs her hair tied back: a hairstyle slot on the head, next.

How she was made

1 · Mainstream picture

Your official Railgun art. Other mainstream pictures supplied the winter blazer and a second view.

2 · T-pose redraw

Qwen image edit redraws her alone, arms straight out, on white. The same edit dresses her in the winter blazer, keeping the same face and body.

3 · Outfit swap

The winter blazer is just another body mesh. It is fitted onto the summer skeleton, scaled per axis over the matching bones; the worst bone sits 1.6 cm off.

4 · Triangles + bones

Hunyuan3D gives the shape (30 steps, about 45 s). It is cut to about 16,000 triangles, and each arm is found by surface connectivity so a raised arm never drags the body.

The white model the hologram is drawn from

What changed since the first trial

  • T-pose instead of A-pose. With the arms hanging, the generated upper arms were fused to the ribs, and raising an arm pulled a sheet of triangles off the hip. In a T-pose the arms are separate, and the T-pose is also VRM's rest pose.
  • No colour work. The hologram draws the model's own triangles: the back ones are hidden by a dark inner body and the front ones glow.
  • One skeleton, many outfits. The head and hair come from one model, the bodies from each outfit model, cut at the neck.
  • Motions are character-independent. They target VRM humanoid bone names, not this mesh.

Next

  • Motion library from text. A text-to-motion model can turn the novel's own action lines ("she flicks the coin") into bone animation, saved as VRM animation files that any figure can play.
  • Outfits per volume. An agent reads each volume, lists what the character wears, and generates the missing outfit meshes onto the same skeleton.
  • The rest of the cast, and a non-anime book to check that the method generalises.
