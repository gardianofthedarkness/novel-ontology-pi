World Engine v2
    Page 1 · Overview

    The world engine, block by block

    A book is read into a world first: who is where, when, in which building, above or under the ground. Dialogue, movement, things, fights and identity are then decided inside that world. This is the second version of the CLI, and these pages go through every block built so far, what it decides and how, how it is tested, and what is missing.

    ~60modules in the world engine
    77vol 1 scenes the world loop has read
    42baked 3D models (Kenney, KayKit)
    6%of a recorded pick was Jev; the rest was replays and Chrome

    The pipeline

    1 · Extraction (the Digger)sentences, scenes, sentence kinds (dialogue, monologue, fact), events as actions and movements, names and identity at birth
    ↓
    2 · Base layerwhat the faculties found before any review: scenes, events, exchanges, monologues, facts, states, roster
    ↓

    ↓
    4 · Places and groundcanvas of places, scale, landscape manifold, city fill, buildings, the underground

    5 · The enginethe world grid, births, gatherings, movement, things, fights and destruction, played event by event
    ↓

    Sentence kinds: dialogues, monologues, facts

    BuiltTestedNot run on vol 1

    Every sentence is spoken, thought, fact, action or narration, and Jev says whether the people talking change there. An exchange runs as long as the same people talk, narration and actions between their lines inside it, and ends when they change or after 8 quiet sentences. A run of thought is a monologue; a run of facts is the world's background knowledge.

    Page 3 · World first

    The world loop

    Scene by scene in reading order, Jev reads each scene in a composed context and says who is there, where, and when; the world keeps what the text cannot break.

    Composed context

    BuiltTestedSeen live

    Every decision gets the same shape: the world as it stands (who is where since when), what the book has told of the world (its latest facts), the story so far, this scene in full, and the decision's own part. Over its 10,000-character budget, the oldest story goes first, then the oldest facts, then the least recently placed people, then the middle of a very long scene.

    Tests

    Gaps

    Presence, place and time

    BuiltTestedSeen live

    One call for who is in the scene (up to 14 candidates: named, event actors, the scene before's people) and where (the place before, places the text names, places the candidates are at, or a new one); one for when it is against the scene before, among the times the laws allow, with the time words of its text. A fragment scene costs nothing and carries the scene before on.

    Decides with
    Jev nouls and choices, about 2 calls a scene (vol 1: 140 calls for 77 scenes)
    Files
    experiments/world-first/run.mjs (289 lines)

    The world's laws

    BuiltTestedSeen live
    • Nobody vanishes: a person stays where last placed until a scene places them elsewhere.
    • Nobody is in two places at once on the one clock; a scene sharing someone with the scene before can only come after it or be a flashback.
    • A flashback is the past: it logs what people did then and moves nobody now.
    • Actors are there: an event's actor the world lacks joins when Jev thought it likely, else is flagged.
    • Things are held by one person; knowledge flows only through being there or hearing it in a dialogue.
    Files
    world.mjs (119 lines), laws.mjs (46 lines)

    Dialogue nodes

    BuiltTestedSeen live

    Each exchange is a node on the scene's timeline: live (two face to face), group (three or more, one box), remote (phone, message, telepathy: one party elsewhere) or public (one voice to everyone). Jev says the kind and the two parties; the world adds a missing live party unless that breaks the two-places law. A monologue costs nothing and moves no one.

    Decides with
    Jev, one call per node (a second for a remote node's local party)

    Review pool and Pi

    BuiltTestedSeen live

Pi answers from the original text (novel source grep|similar); between scenes the world turns answers into pins and replays from the start (at most 6 replays; the cache keeps unchanged scenes free). A landscape answer is pinned for the stage without a replay.

    The underground

    Halls carved in the ground that the soil or rock around them holds up, not buildings. They are generated with each landscape; the subway follows its own logic; and an area's underground only comes alive once someone goes down there.

    Ground bands and halls

    BuiltTested

    Soil to 12 m, weathered rock to 40 m, hard rock below, each with the span it can hold. Hall kinds: subway tunnel, station and passage; underground mall; storm-water tank and channel; deep lab, military and quantum facilities. Pillars stand where a roof would span too far.

    Files
    src/ground/stage/underground.ts (249 lines)
    Docs

    Three depth choices

    BuiltTested

    Jev picks one of three overlapping intervals, so it is never asked to tell many levels apart: shallow (0–25 m: basements, car parks, malls), subway (5–20 m, by kind) and deep (20–110 m). A box on the chosen interval's picture finds the hall. Measured over six cities, the box lands in the right hall 94–96% (shallow), 99% (subway), about 99% (deep).

    Tests

    The subway

    BuiltTested

    Buildings and identity

    The same person or building named several ways must be one thing in the world, or the two-places law misfires and the city fills with copies.

    Building EM

    BuiltTestedSeen live

    For every building a scene mentions, Jev answers which it is: the building the scene happens in, one of the book's named buildings so far, the subway, another building of its own, or not a building here; and whether its name is a proper name. Answers join mentions, spots and named places into one building each. A named building joins only at 60% or more, so weak answers never chain buildings together; a district's own name is never a building.

    Movement: spots and paths

    BuiltTestedSeen live

    Page 10 · Measured 2026-10-30

    What structured input changes

    Jev reads a state that can be a JSON object; questions point into it; a request has no limit on questions, only on size (64k tokens), and is billed per input token. Every test below ran on vol 1 with Jev 1.14.

    StepBeforeStructuredWhat changed
    Sentence kinds, 300 sentences1.1 s · 8 requests0.25 s · 3 requests86% same kinds; quoted lines now spoken, not thought
    Dialogue exchanges, 300 sentences4.1 s · 37 chained0.25 s · 3 at once14 exchanges both; median 6 → 11 sentences
    Movement, 36 events22.8 s on pictures4.4 s · no Chromeend-spot confidence 0.25 → 0.93; moves agree 91%, way 96%
    A whole scene in one request4.2 s · 36 requests0.6 s · 4 requestsless sure (0.46 against 0.92): keep the chain
    Units, then destinations—3.5 s units · 0.9 s destinations12 movers in 11 units; one destination per unit

    Where the time goes

    A recorded pick of 36 events took 449 s. Jev answered in 28 s of it. Before each event question the loop replayed the engine from the line's start (1.5 s by scene 4) and reopened the 3D page in headless Chrome (3.7 s) to draw the picture it asks on. A full-line load measures about 15 s (engine 9.8 s, Chrome 5 s), so most of the 222 s before the first event question is something else, to be timed.

    Readings worth keeping

    • On a rendered picture Jev read pinned spots barely better than chance (0.25 with 8 spots); with spots listed by id and description it followed the text: the rooftop when she jumps onto a five-storey building, the plaza when he runs across it carrying the girl, staying put when people only talk.
    • Units got the clear cases right (carrying, grabbing and jumping) and two wrong: a unit kept after one member leaves the other behind, and a person paired with her own title (an identity miss).
    • Per-unit destinations as parallel requests (0.9 s) were no faster than one request holding them all (1.0 s); parallel requests earn their place when each unit needs its own picture for a box.
    Page 11 · Going forward

    The v2 design

    The full text is docs/v2/world-engine-v2.md. In short: every Jev request reads a document, and the world, not pictures, carries what the engine already knows.

    Rules for every request

    Proposed
    • The state is a JSON document: definitions once, items keyed by id, the decision's part last.
    • Questions are short and point into it; a short label per option, not its definition again.
    • One request per decision step; independent steps at once.
    • Pictures only where the picture is the evidence: boxes and the model catalog.
    • Chains where answers depend on the last; fan out where they do not.

    Movement: units, ways, destinations

    Proposed

    Per event: (1) one request for who moves and who or what each moves with (people and movable things), joined into units; (2) each unit's way; (3) each unit's destination, all at once: a choice over the place's spots on the structured state, or a box on that unit's own picture when it goes to a room, a roof or underground (one request per such unit, in parallel); (4) the engine plays the event and each person's spot, from the engine, is the next event's state. Identities are settled before units.

    Things that move

    Proposed

    Vehicles, mechs and other nominated objects listed per scene by the objects step, kept in the roster beside the characters, and able to join a unit.
